What is the Volume Shader BM Benchmark?

The Volume Shader BM Benchmark is a specialized browser-based GPU evaluation tool designed to gauge the computational depth and arithmetic efficiency of modern graphics processors. Originally adapted from high-end demoscene rendering routines and raymarching algorithms, the benchmark produces an intricate, continuous 3D volumetric smoke and plasma cloud inside a WebGL viewport.

Unlike conventional polygonal rasterization tests that evaluate triangle throughput and vertex transformation speeds (e.g., standard game meshes), Volume Shader BM relies almost entirely on the GPU's fragment processing pipeline. By stepping through 3D space along line-of-sight rays and accumulating color, density, and light transmittance at dozens or hundreds of discrete intervals per screen pixel, it creates an aggressive synthetic thermal and computational workload for desktop and mobile GPUs alike.

How Does Volumetric Raymarching Stress Hardware?

Traditional 3D gaming pipelines render surfaces as hollow shells composed of triangles. Volumetric rendering, on the other hand, deals with objects that have no distinct solid surfaces—such as atmospheric clouds, nebulae, fluid dynamics, and medical CT scans.

1. Ray Casting

A vector is cast from the virtual camera position through each viewport pixel coordinate straight into 3D Cartesian coordinates.

2. Procedural Noise

At every sampling step, the shader computes multi-octave Perlin or Simplex noise algorithms to evaluate dynamic fluid density.

3. Light Integral

Beer-Lambert law formulations are computed continuously to simulate light extinction, dispersion, and secondary light scattering.

Running this calculation across 1920x1080 (or higher) pixels at 60 or 120 times per second means the GPU performs hundreds of millions of transcendental mathematical operations per second. This makes the Volume Shader BM Benchmark one of the most effective synthetic stress tests for diagnosing GPU thermal throttling, unstable overclocks, and memory bus congestion right inside Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, Safari, and Microsoft Edge.

How to Optimize Your Browser for Maximum GPU Performance

  • Enable Hardware Acceleration: Open browser settings, search for "Hardware Acceleration," and make sure "Use graphics acceleration when available" is toggled on.
  • Configure High-Performance GPU Profile: On Windows laptops with dual graphics (Intel/AMD integrated + NVIDIA discrete), verify in Windows Graphics Settings that your browser is assigned to "High Performance".
  • Unlock the FPS Cap (60Hz limit): If your benchmark will not exceed 60 FPS on high refresh displays, disable Vertical Sync in your graphics driver settings or run Chromium with --disable-frame-rate-limit.
  • Monitor Thermal Throttling: If your FPS drops sharply after 10–15 seconds of testing, your GPU or CPU may be reducing clock speeds due to elevated operating temperatures.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is the Volume Shader BM Benchmark safe for my GPU?

Yes. Although it pushes your graphics hardware to high fragment utilization, it operates within the standard sandboxed environment of your web browser via standard WebGL calls. It cannot modify voltages or bypass internal hardware safety mechanisms.

Can I run this benchmark on an iPhone or Android device?

Yes! The benchmark engine is fully responsive and supports WebGL 1.0 and 2.0 implementations on mobile Safari, Chrome for Android, and mobile Firefox.

Why does my discrete GPU name show as "ANGLE"?

Most Chromium-based browsers use Google's ANGLE (Almost Native Graphics Layer Engine) translation layer to convert WebGL API calls into native Direct3D 11/12 (on Windows) or Metal (on macOS). Your actual physical GPU model is typically listed alongside the ANGLE renderer string.

About Us

VolumeShaderBM Benchmark is an open initiative spearheaded by web graphics enthusiasts, shader developers, and computer hardware analysts. Our primary mission is to deliver zero-install, platform-agnostic GPU evaluation software that empowers consumers to verify display drivers, detect hardware throttling, and quantify real-time rendering performance right inside the browser.

We adhere strictly to open WebGL and WebGPU development standards, avoiding bloatware or closed proprietary wrappers. All computations are conducted entirely on your local machine.

Privacy Policy

At volumeshaderbmbenchmark.github.io, we prioritize the protection of your personal privacy:

  • No Personal Data Harvesting: We do not ask for, harvest, or store personal identifiers, names, email addresses, or IP logs.
  • Local GPU Processing: All shader code execution, FPS calculations, and score generation occur solely in client-side memory (your web browser). No frame buffers or render results are uploaded to external cloud servers.
  • Hardware String Query: Unmasked WebGL parameters (such as the unmasked renderer and vendor string) are queried locally solely to display your GPU model in the HUD.
  • Third-Party Libraries & Cookies: We utilize trusted, industry-standard Content Delivery Networks (CDNs) for CSS and icon distribution. We do not place invasive tracking or behavioral advertising cookies on your machine.
